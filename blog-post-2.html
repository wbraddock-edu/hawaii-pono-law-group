Hawaii Landlord-Tenant Law Explained

Real Estate · May 30, 2025 · 6 min read

Hawaii's landlord-tenant law is governed primarily by Hawaii Revised Statutes Chapter 521 (the Residential Landlord-Tenant Code), which has some provisions that differ significantly from mainland states. Whether you're a landlord or a tenant in Hawaii, understanding your rights and responsibilities is essential.

Security Deposits

In Hawaii, landlords may collect a security deposit of up to one month's rent (for unfurnished units). The landlord must return the deposit within 14 days of the tenancy ending, along with an itemized statement of any deductions. Failure to return the deposit on time can result in the landlord losing the right to make any deductions and potentially being liable for damages. This is one of the most common sources of landlord-tenant disputes in Hawaii.

Rent Increases

Hawaii law does not impose statewide rent control, but some counties have enacted temporary measures. Landlords must provide proper notice before increasing rent — the required notice period equals the rent payment period (e.g., month-to-month tenants must receive 45 days' notice of a rent increase). For fixed-term leases, rent cannot be increased until the lease expires unless the lease specifically allows it.

Landlord Entry

A landlord must provide at least two days' written notice before entering a tenant's rental unit, except in genuine emergencies. This is a commonly misunderstood right — many Hawaii tenants don't realize their landlord cannot simply enter their home at will, even for repairs or inspections.

Eviction (Summary Possession)

Hawaii's eviction process (called summary possession) has specific steps that must be followed exactly. For non-payment of rent, a landlord must first serve a written notice giving the tenant 5 days to pay or vacate. For lease violations, the notice period varies by violation type. Self-help evictions (changing locks, removing belongings, shutting off utilities) are illegal in Hawaii regardless of the circumstances.

Tenant Remedies for Habitability Issues

Hawaii landlords have a legal duty to maintain rental properties in habitable condition. If a landlord fails to make necessary repairs after proper written notice, tenants may have the right to withhold rent, repair and deduct, or terminate the lease. These remedies have specific procedural requirements — consult an attorney before withholding rent, as an improperly executed rent withholding can backfire legally.
