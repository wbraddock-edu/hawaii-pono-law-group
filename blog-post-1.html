How to Find a Lawyer in Honolulu

Legal Guide · Jun 16, 2025 · 6 min read

Finding the right attorney for your specific legal needs can feel overwhelming. The Hawaii bar has hundreds of licensed attorneys in Honolulu alone, covering dozens of practice areas. This guide will help you find qualified legal help efficiently.

Start with Your Specific Need

The most important step is identifying your specific legal issue. Law is highly specialized — an excellent divorce attorney may have little expertise in business law, and a skilled real estate lawyer may not be the right choice for an immigration matter. Be specific about what you need: "I need to form an LLC," "I'm going through a divorce and we have children," "My landlord is refusing to return my security deposit." This specificity will guide you to the right type of attorney.

The Hawaii State Bar Association

The Hawaii State Bar Association (HSBA) at hsba.org maintains a lawyer referral service that can connect you with attorneys in your practice area. The Hawaii Lawyer Referral Service offers initial consultations at a reduced fee, making it accessible for those uncertain whether they even need legal help. This is one of the most reliable starting points for finding qualified counsel.

Free Legal Aid Resources

If cost is a barrier, there are significant free and low-cost legal resources in Hawaii. Legal Aid Society of Hawaii provides free civil legal assistance to low-income individuals. The Hawaii Volunteer Lawyers Project offers pro bono services for qualifying individuals. The UH Richardson School of Law's clinics provide supervised legal services in family law, immigration, and other areas. Income qualification varies — contact these organizations directly for eligibility.

Questions to Ask a Prospective Attorney

During any initial consultation, ask: How long have you practiced this type of law in Hawaii? Have you handled cases similar to mine? How do you structure fees — hourly, flat fee, or contingency? Who in your office will primarily handle my case? How do you prefer to communicate with clients? What is your honest assessment of my situation? The answers will tell you a great deal about fit and competence.

Red Flags to Watch For

Be cautious of attorneys who guarantee specific outcomes (no ethical attorney can do this), who are unavailable for weeks at a time, who can't explain their fee structure clearly, or who seem unfamiliar with Hawaii-specific law in your practice area. A good attorney will be honest about uncertainty and the limits of their knowledge.
