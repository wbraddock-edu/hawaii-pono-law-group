Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Pono Law Group and our legal services.

Yes. We offer a free 30-minute initial consultation for new clients. This gives you the opportunity to describe your situation and receive an honest assessment of your options before making any commitment. Consultations are available in-person, by phone, or by video conference.
Our fee structure varies by matter type. Most litigation and family law cases are billed hourly. Estate planning and business formation services often use flat fees for defined scope of work. Some personal injury matters use contingency fees. We will discuss fees clearly during your consultation and provide a written engagement agreement before beginning any work.
Yes. All attorneys at Pono Law Group are licensed by the Hawaii State Bar and in good standing. Our attorneys are also admitted to the U.S. District Court for the District of Hawaii and the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals for federal matters.
Yes. We handle matters across all Hawaiian islands. For cases on neighbor islands, we appear in circuit courts on Maui, Kauai, and the Big Island as needed. Many client meetings and consultations can be conducted by phone or video for those on other islands.
Our team includes attorneys and staff who are conversational or fluent in Japanese, Tagalog, and Ilocano. For other languages, we work with qualified interpreters to ensure clear communication with all clients.
Case timelines vary enormously by matter type and complexity. An uncontested divorce in Hawaii can be completed in 3–6 months. A contested child custody case can take 1–2 years. Estate planning documents can often be completed in 2–4 weeks. We give each client a realistic timeline estimate at the outset and update you as circumstances change.
For an initial consultation, bring any documents relevant to your matter: marriage/divorce papers, property deeds, business formation documents, leases, contracts, correspondence. Even if you don't have everything, a consultation is still productive — we can advise you on what documentation to gather.
Yes. Attorney-client privilege is one of the most fundamental protections in the legal system. Everything you share with our attorneys — including during an initial consultation — is confidential and protected by privilege. We cannot disclose what you tell us without your permission, with very narrow exceptions required by law.
Yes. All Pono Law Group attorneys commit to pro bono service as part of our professional and community obligations. We work with Hawaii Volunteer Lawyers Project and Legal Aid Society of Hawaii on qualifying cases. If you cannot afford legal representation, we encourage you to contact these organizations to explore whether you qualify for pro bono assistance.
