Small Business Legal Basics for Hawaii LLCs

Business Law · May 12, 2025 · 6 min read

Forming and operating a small business in Hawaii requires attention to legal fundamentals that vary in some important ways from other states. Whether you're opening a food truck, a tourism business, or a professional services firm, here are the essential legal steps for Hawaii entrepreneurs.

Choosing the Right Business Structure

Most small Hawaii businesses choose between a sole proprietorship, LLC (Limited Liability Company), or corporation. The LLC is the most popular choice for small businesses because it provides personal liability protection while being relatively simple to form and operate. Hawaii uses the term "LLLC" (Limited Liability Limited Company) for certain partnerships, which can be confusing — consult an attorney to determine the right structure for your specific situation.

Forming an LLC in Hawaii

To form an LLC in Hawaii, you file Articles of Organization with the Hawaii Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs (DCCA) Business Registration Division and pay a $50 filing fee. You'll also need to file an annual report ($15/year) and maintain a registered agent in Hawaii. The process is relatively straightforward, but having an attorney draft a thorough Operating Agreement — the document that governs how your LLC operates — is worth the investment, particularly if you have multiple members.

General Excise Tax

Hawaii's General Excise Tax (GET) is one of the most important business taxes to understand. Unlike a traditional sales tax (which consumers pay), the GET is technically a tax on the privilege of doing business in Hawaii and is paid by the business on gross revenues. The standard rate is 4% (4.5% in Honolulu County). Most Hawaii businesses pass this cost to customers by adding it to invoices, but this must be disclosed properly.

Licenses and Permits

Beyond the basic business registration, many Hawaii businesses require additional licenses. Food businesses need Health Department permits. Contractors need DCCA licensing. Tour operators need permits for certain activities. Financial, medical, and legal professionals need professional licensing. Retail businesses in tourist areas often need county permits. A business attorney or the Hawaii DCCA's BREG office can help you identify the specific permits your business requires.

Employment Law Essentials

If you hire employees in Hawaii, you must comply with Hawaii's employment laws, which are generally more employee-protective than federal minimums. Hawaii has its own minimum wage, mandatory paid sick leave, and prepaid health care requirements (Hawaii is one of only a few states that requires employers to provide health insurance to employees working 20+ hours/week). Non-compliance with Hawaii employment law is a common source of small business legal liability.
